DRC SEIR Model Check

One example outbreak on the Ituri and Nord-Kivu health zones, checked six ways. Every check compares epymorph's output with something computed separately, from the model's equations or from SEIR theory, without using epymorph's code.

The example

Results

Check 1Transitions happen at the rates in the equations

What should happen

On every time step, in every zone, epymorph draws each transition from a Poisson distribution whose mean is the SEIR rate times the step length. Infection uses everyone present in the zone at that moment, visitors included: β·S·I/N.

How we checked

From one run's tick-by-tick output we rebuilt the state just before each step, computed the expected count for each transition ourselves, and compared it with what epymorph drew. If the draws are right, the standardized differences (observed − expected)/√expected follow a standard normal curve.

Check 2Travel follows the distance kernel

What should happen

Each day, 5% of each zone's residents (rounded down) pick a destination with probability proportional to exp(−distance / φ), counting their own zone. They spend the first third of the day there and are all home for the rest.

How we checked

We computed great-circle distances and the expected number of visitors arriving at and leaving each zone with our own code, then compared that with the people present in each zone on the first step of every day. On the second step, everyone should be back home.

Check 3The average run matches our own implementation

What should happen

Averaged over many runs, a stochastic model should track the deterministic version of the same equations. Small gaps are expected from chance in the earliest days and from rounding travelers down to whole people.

How we checked

We wrote the model again from scratch in plain numpy: once advancing in the same two steps per day, and once as a continuous-time ODE solved with fine RK4 steps. Both start from the same 300 infectious people.

Check 4Early growth matches SEIR theory

What should happen

Before many people are immune, cases grow exponentially at the rate r that solves (1 + r/σ)(1 + r/γ) = R0. Movement doesn't change r here, because every zone has the same R0.

How we checked

We fitted a straight line to log daily cases over the window shown, for the average of the runs and for each run separately, and compared the slopes with theory.

Check 5The final size matches theory

What should happen

With no immunity at the start and no interventions, an SEIR epidemic with R0 = 2 ends with the share z ever infected, where z = 1 − e−2z. That gives the same answer in every zone, whatever its size.

How we checked

We ran long enough for the epidemic to burn out, then compared each zone's share ever infected (median and 90% range across runs) with the theoretical value.

Check 6No one is created or lost

What should happen

Movement and disease only move people between places and compartments. Every zone's residents should add up to its GRID3 population at every step, and no compartment should go negative.

How we checked

We summed the compartments for every zone at every step of every run.
